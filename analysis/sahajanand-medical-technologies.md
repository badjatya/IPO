## What the company does
Surat-based, R&D-led maker of Class III cardiovascular implants (founded 2001 by Dhirajlal Kotadia): drug-eluting stents (flagship Supraflex Cruz), balloon catheters, DCBs, plus structural-heart devices (Hydra TAVI, Cocoon occluders). FY26 revenue mix: vascular intervention 61.1%, structural heart 16.8%, others (traded goods, renal stent, peripheral DCB) 22.1%. Geography FY26: Europe 38.7%, RoW 35.2%, India 26.1% (India was 34.8% in FY24). Per Frost & Sullivan (company-paid) it holds ~25% of India DES volume and is top-5 in DES in Germany, Spain, Poland, Brazil. Three plants (two India, one Thailand), 65–77 countries, 119 patents granted, 75 clinical studies. Growth partly via acquisitions (Imex Spain, Zarek Brazil, Vascular Concepts, Vascular Innovations).

## Red flags
- 100% OFS with PE funds (Samara, Kotak Pre-IPO, NHPEA) among sellers — exit, no growth or debt-reduction capital
- Profitability only recently positive (FY24 loss); FY26 PAT helped by tax credits; premium P/E (~53x) against 14% EBITDA margin
- Leverage and cash: net debt/EBITDA 4.89x at Jun-26, Q1 operating cash flow negative, 138 working-capital days
- Law-enforcement notices: Delhi Police (Boston Ivy Healthcare, 2025) and CBI Anti-Corruption (stent supplies to RML Hospital via Zunison Healthcare, Jan 2026) have sought documents from the company
- Regulatory: Brazil ANVISA cancelled a product registration; Hydra TAVI rejected in Taiwan and Australia; USFDA effort abandoned; most CE certificates are under MDD and valid only to 31 Dec 2027 pending EU MDR transition; ~39% revenue from Europe
- Low utilisation of newer lines (DCB 1.8%, TAVI 19.8% in FY26) and India share of revenue shrinking (34.8% → 26.1%)
- Industry data from a company-paid Frost & Sullivan report; DES price-control risk in India (not quantified in extract); one promoter has limited experience in the business

## Verdict
**Apply for listing gains only (small, GMP-dependent).**
1. Real franchise: ~25% India DES share, 16% revenue CAGR, margins and PAT improving, strong Europe traction.
2. GMP ₹40–46 (~12–13%) and a QIB-heavy book offer a listing pop if anchors are good; retail quota is likely only ~10%, so allotment odds are low.
3. Against that: 53x P/E vs 43.9x peer average, thin margins, leverage, pure OFS and open investigation notices cap the upside.
Long-term: good product moat but expensive and cash-hungry; wait for margin and cash-flow proof before holding.
