as provide liquidity and a public market for the Equity Shares in India.

and Statutory Disclosures – Authority for the Offer” on page 443.

Financial Year 2026. Most of our large funds are in or entering in their third and fourth series, and we have
consistently been able to raise capital across the series, indicating our ability to generate sustainable performance,
maintain client confidence, and adapt to market conditions. In the last three Financial Years and the three months
period ended June 30, 2026, we raised capital commitment aggregating to ₹475,262.50 million, deployed
investments aggregating to ₹382,293.99 million and realized investments aggregating to ₹312,329.40 million.
Most of our funds also have a track record of delivering strong performance consistently across different market
cycles. Such strong realizations of our funds ensured the return of realized portion of NAV back to clients, thereby
establishing a track record of delivering strong performance. We have invested significant time and resources over
the years in building a global, scalable, coverage platform for a diverse client base including institutional investors,
ultra-high net worth individuals (“UHNIs”), high net worth individuals (“HNIs”) and family offices. As of June
30, 2026, through the funds that we managed, we had 11,009 client relationships globally and in India, including

the trust and confidence that our clients have in our platform and investment strategies, as well as our consistent

invests in a mix of equity, quasi-equity and debt instruments and (ii) private credit (“Private Credit”), which
primarily invests in debt or convertible debt instruments and secular instruments for superior risk adjusted returns.
Our wholly owned subsidiary, Sekura India Management Limited (“Sekura”), is part of our — asset operating
and management team and proficiently supports our various business verticals, including operations, maintenance,
monitoring, efficiency improvement, and turnaround management. We are an experienced player, catering to a
diverse client base of global and domestic institutional clients and manage India focused funds across our business
verticals. As of June 30, 2026, our FPAUM totaled ₹486,231.76 million. Over last three financial years, our
FPAUM grew at compounded annual growth rate (“CAGR”) of approximately 23.43% to ₹447,104.42 million in
Financial Year 2026. Most of our large funds are in or entering in their third and fourth series, and we have
consistently been able to raise capital across the series, indicating our ability to generate sustainable performance,
maintain client confidence, and adapt to market conditions. In the last three Financial Years and the three months
period ended June 30, 2026, we raised capital commitment aggregating to ₹475,262.50 million, deployed
investments aggregating to ₹382,293.99 million and realized investments aggregating to ₹312,329.40 million.
Most of our funds also have a track record of delivering strong performance consistently across different market
cycles. Such strong realizations of our funds ensured the return of realized portion of NAV back to clients, thereby
establishing a track record of delivering strong performance. We have invested significant time and resources over
the years in building a global, scalable, coverage platform for a diverse client base including institutional investors,
ultra-high net worth individuals (“UHNIs”), high net worth individuals (“HNIs”) and family offices. As of June
30, 2026, through the funds that we managed, we had 11,009 client relationships globally and in India, including
1,493 repeat clients (i.e., clients who have invested in more than one of our funds) and 8,800 clients added from

Significant Factors Affecting our Results of Operations
The results of our operations and our financial condition are significantly affected by a number of factors, many
of which may be beyond our control, including our assets under management, revenue model, regulatory
landscape and policies, personnel and operating costs and industry competition.
Growth and our performance of our assets under management
Our results of operations are materially affected by our AUM. Our ability to grow our revenues and income in our
business verticals depends on our ability to attract new capital and clients and our ability to successfully invest
our funds’ capital. Good investment performance increases the attractiveness of our products with clients resulting
in higher inflows and a consequent increase in our revenues. Hence, events adversely impacting such investment
performance may adversely affect our business. Furthermore, we raise capital from multiple geographies and are
subject to various factors that affect conducting business internationally. These factors include the economic and
political conditions in these regions, which might also impact our business. While we continue to explore
opportunities to expand our business through organic and inorganic modes, we cannot be certain that the growth
witnessed by us in the past will be maintained in the future.
Our revenue model
We derive our Total Income primarily from three income streams: (i) Management and Advisory Fees; (ii)
Variable Additional Return; (iii) GP Income; and (iv) Miscellaneous Income. A significant portion of Total
Income comes from our Management and Advisory Fees. Management and Advisory Fee is the fee received for
providing management and advisory services. Management and Advisory Fee is generally charged as a percentage
of capital committed, capital invested, net asset value, gross block etc. Our Management and Advisory Fees stream
is notably stable, largely stemming from multi-year contractual agreements.
Variable Additional Return is the additional variable return to the Company and its Subsidiaries once the fund has
achieved the hurdle rate of return for its investors based on NAV and/or Realisation, shown as other operating
revenues in Restated Consolidated Financial Information. The recognition of Variable Additional Return is guided
by the waterfall distribution mechanism specified in the respective fund documentation and as per Company and
its Subsidiaries’ policies. Variable Additional Return is the additional return on sponsor or general partner
commitment, which is dependent on the performance of our funds. Based on the guidance under the Ind AS and
the current policy that we adopted for recognition of Variable Additional Return, Variable Additional Return is
recognized in our books of accounts after a minimum of three years from its initial close date. It is also contingent
upon the fund’s performance achieving at least 1.25 times the hurdle rate specified in the respective fund
documents at the time of recognition of Variable Additional Return. According to the waterfall mechanism
specified in each fund document, and subject to the above conditions, Variable Additional Return is calculated
and recognized over the remaining life of the respective funds up to the expected date of the first realisation and
as per Company’s policy.
GP Income refers to income on investments in funds or co-investing in portfolio companies, on the same terms as
the primary fund. It is calculated as sum of Interest income on debt instruments/ Pass through certificates and Net
Gain / (Loss) on fair value changes from investments in AIF/ Debt Instrument / Pass through certificate / equity
forming part of other income in the Restated Consolidated Financial Information. General partner and/or sponsor
investments reflect our alignment of interests with clients by using our capital in the funds or co-investing in
portfolio companies, on the same terms as the primary fund. ‘GP Income’ represents the income on these
investments.
