Overview
Our Company is the largest single coal producing company in India in terms of production in Fiscal 2026, accounting for 21.0%
of the domestic coal production (source: CRISIL Report). As at April 1, 2026, the Talcher and Ib Valley coalfields held a total
estimated coal resource of approximately 106.76 billion tonnes (source: CRISIL Report). As at April 1, 2026, our audited coal
reserves as per the JORC Code stood at 9,840.31 MT, which would sustain our production operations for around 45 years
considering the present production level (the Annual Coal Production of 218.31 MT in Fiscal 2026) (source: DMT Report).
Assuming that there will be a production increase year on year with a CAGR of 4.70%, which is based on the production for
the past 10 years, these coal reserves would sustain for around 25 years (source: DMT Report). Assuming a production rate
similar to the current level is maintained, the additional resource-to-reserve conversion has the potential to extend the
operational life of our mines by approximately 100 years in addition to the 45 years of audited coal reserve life (assuming a
constant rate of production at Fiscal 2026 production levels, as per the DMT Report) (source: CRISIL Report). Assuming the
production increases at a CAGR of 4.70%, which was the CAGR in our production from Fiscal 2016 to Fiscal 2026, the
additional resource-to-reserve conversion has the potential to extend the operational life of our mines by approximately 20 years
in addition to the 25 years of audited coal reserve life (assuming coal production growth at a CAGR 4.70%, as per the DMT
Report) (source: CRISIL Report). However, our actual mine life extension will depend on the success of future exploration
programs, reserve conversion rates, mining and environmental approvals, and future production levels and the demand for non-
coking coal (source: CRISIL Report). Our Resources are predominantly thermal-grade non-coking coal (source: DMT Report).
Our primary product is non-coking coal. We produce various grades of non-coking coal, including washed / beneficiated coal.
We cater to the power utilities sector, including state power utilities, private power utilities, captive power plants and
independent power producers, as well as non-power industries, including the cement sector and the sponge iron sector. Our vast
  225
resource base and significant reserves, coupled with our strategic location in the Talcher and Ib Valley coalfields, Odisha,
provide us with a steady and reliable supply of non-coking coal.
Our Company is a wholly owned subsidiary of Coal India Limited and was conferred with Miniratna Category I status on
December 3, 2019. Our Company was incorporated on April 3, 1992, carving out of South Eastern Coalfields Limited in order
to fulfill the growing energy requirement of the country by enhancing coal production from two coalfields, namely the Talcher
and Ib Valley coalfields, Odisha. We are the largest coal producer among Coal India Limited’s subsidiaries, accounting for
28.4% of Coal India Limited’s total coal production in Fiscal 2026 (source: CRISIL Report).
We have expanded our operations significantly over the years, with our coal production increasing 5.92% from 206.10 million

We conduct advance overburden removal, deploying equipment to maintain adequate coal exposure to ensure coal availability
for subsequent production periods, eliminating delays between stripping and extraction phases, and optimizing utilization of
heavy earth-moving machinery and other equipment. Surface miners enable us to extract coal of size less than 100mm without
the use of explosives, resulting in reduced ground vibration, dust and noise pollution, and better coal quality through selective
mining. We have increased our deployment of surface miners, and as a result, our production through surface miners as a
percentage of our total coal production increased to 99.37% in Fiscal 2026. As at September 30, 2026, we deployed 74 surface
miners in our operations, comprising 22 owned and 52 contracted surface miners. As at March 31, 2026, we owned 22 surface
miners out of Coal India Limited’s total fleet of 47 surface miners, the largest surface miner fleet among Coal India Limited’s
subsidiaries (source: CRISIL Report).
The abovementioned has been effective, as our coal production registered a CAGR of 4.70% from Fiscal 2016 to Fiscal 2026.
Our predominantly opencast mining profile, extensive mechanisation and surface miner deployment have enabled us to
maintain the lowest production cost per tonne among Coal India Limited’s subsidiaries in Fiscal 2026 (source: CRISIL Report).

holdings of 3,316.65 hectares, whether vested, owned or leased-in, are predominantly utilized for colonies and related
infrastructure.

power accounts for approximately 70% of India’s electricity generation in Fiscal 2026 and continues to serve as the backbone
of the country's electricity system, providing reliable baseload power and supporting grid stability (source: CRISIL Report).
The demand for non-coking coal in India increased from 1,112 million tonnes in Fiscal 2025 to 1,186 million tonnes in Fiscal
2026 (source: CRISIL Report). Coal demand from power plants in India stood at 698 million tonnes in Fiscal 2025 and is
expected to reach 843 million tonnes by Fiscal 2031, driven by the growth of the power industry (source: CRISIL Report). The
growth in non-coking coal demand in India will likely continue until Fiscal 2036, with a projected demand of 1,564 million
tonnes in Fiscal 2036 (source: CRISIL Report). While renewable energy capacity is expected to increase significantly,
renewable power generation remains subject to intermittency and variability arising from weather patterns and seasonal
conditions. As a result, thermal power generation is expected to continue to play an important balancing role in ensuring
reliability of electricity supply, particularly during periods of peak demand and when renewable generation is unavailable or
insufficient (source: CRISIL Report).
After Fiscal 2036, demand for non-coking coal in India may decline due to the emergence of renewable energy as an alternative
source of energy (source: CRISIL Report). Demand for non-coking coal from the power sector is estimated at 934.40 million
tonnes in Fiscal 2035 (source: CRISIL Report). It is projected to decline to 933.50 million tonnes in Fiscal 2036, 930.00 million
tonnes in Fiscal 2037 and 927.80 million tonnes in Fiscal 2038. This represents a year-on-year decline of (0.10)%, (0.38)% and
(0.24)% respectively, or a CAGR of (0.24)% between Fiscal 2035 and Fiscal (source: CRISIL Report).
In addition, according to long-term scenarios published by NITI Aayog, India’s coal demand (both coking coal and non-coking
coal) may vary significantly depending on the policy pathway adopted (source: CRISIL Report). Under the Government’s
current policy scenario, coal demand is projected to increase to approximately 2.62 billion tonnes by 2050 and remain at about
1.80 billion tonnes by 2070, whereas under a net zero policy pathway, coal demand is projected to peak at approximately 1.83
billion tonnes in 2050, before declining to about 161 million tonnes by 2070 (source: CRISIL Report).

Demand for non-coking coal, which continues to play a critical role in India’s energy sector and remains the primary
fuel for electricity generation
Our revenues are significantly influenced by demand for non-coking coal from the power generation sector. Additionally, we
produce washed coal, which is primarily used for high-efficiency electricity generation in power stations, enabling power
generation companies to meet strict environmental standards (source: CRISIL Report). Washed coal reduces fuel quantity
requirements (handled and transported) and reduces cost of rail/port transportation for the same energy value (source: CRISIL
Report). It enhances utilization of installed generation capacity and it also reduces ash volume in both pre-combustion and post
combustion stages, which in turn reduces the land requirement for ash disposal (source: CRISIL Report). Washed coal has a
lower ash content in order to support the operations of high-efficiency power stations.
