Qualitative factors
Some of the qualitative factors and our strengths which form the basis for computing the Offer Price are as follows:
•  Our unique business model enables us to serve multiple demand pools and makes us resilient across economic
  cycles
  o  Our end-to-end manufacturing platform, powered by our proprietary Zetwerk OS, transforms fragmented
  capacity across 6,979 third-party suppliers in 26 countries, including India, and our 26 manufacturing
  facilities across four countries, including India, as of Fiscal 2026, into a unified network that allows us to
  serve multiple industries, manufacture diverse product types, and operate across geographies reliably and at
  scale.
  o  We have built a broad base of suppliers across 26 countries, expanding from a solely third-party supplier
  model to a hybrid model incorporating both third parties and our own manufacturing capabilities.
•  Ability to scale in a capital-efficient manner
  o  Our hybrid, technology-driven manufacturing platform combines the advantages of both models, allowing us
  to benefit from the scalability of a distributed network, with capacity accessed primarily through third-party
  manufacturers without a corresponding increase in invested capital, together with the control of selective own
  capacity for complex, high-value manufacturing. Our selective expansion into own manufacturing facilities
  has also allowed us to capture higher margins.
  o  Our hybrid model also supports simultaneous manufacturing, where our proprietary Zetwerk OS coordinates
  a single order across multiple facilities to shorten production cycles. It automates key decisions and replaces
  manual processes with technology. This creates meaningful operating leverage in our cost structure that
  improves with scale.
•  Zetwerk OS, our proprietary technology enables end‑to-end workflow management across the manufacturing
  lifecycle while driving powerful network effects across our operations
  o  Our manufacturing platform operates on our proprietary Zetwerk OS, an end-to-end technology that
  standardises every stage of the manufacturing lifecycle and supports distributed manufacturing. Our
  manufacturing platform integrates modules covering enquiry management, supplier recommendation and
  evaluation, design for manufacturing, order management, production planning, quality management,
  inventory tracking and logistics coordination.
  o  Zetwerk OS is purpose-built for platform-scale workflow, managing thousands of concurrent projects across
  multiple suppliers, geographies and product categories
•  Deep relationships with customers driving higher revenue visibility and stickiness
  o  Many of our customer relationships evolve from single‑order engagements to multi‑year contracts. This is

F.  Comparison of accounting ratios with listed industry peers
  Global manufacturing capacity is organized through operating models that differ in asset ownership, and coordination
  intensity. Each model represents a distinct way of converting supplier capability into finished industrial output.
  •  Vertically Integrated (Captive) Manufacturing: Under this model, company own and operate dedicated
  production facilities designed around specific product lines or process capabilities, placing asset ownership
  primarily within the firm. Production planning, procurement, quality control, and logistics are managed
  internally within the organization. Supplier relationships exist primarily at the raw-material or subcomponent
  level, while final assembly and critical processes remain within captive plants. Capacity expansion occurs
  through direct capital investment, and operational decisions are governed by internal production schedules.
  •  Contract Manufacturing: In this model, a specialized manufacturing firm owns and operates production
  facilities, while producing goods on behalf of external brands. Asset ownership is concentrated with the
  manufacturer, whereas product design, intellectual property, branding, and market access remain with the
  client company. Coordination between the brand and manufacturer is bilateral and contract driven. The brand
  defines product specifications, quality standards, delivery timelines, and volume forecasts, while the contract
  manufacturer executes production within agreed parameters. Capacity expansion is driven by long-term client
  commitments, demand visibility, or aggregation of orders across multiple brands, and typically involves
  investments in new production lines, automation, geographic expansion, or facility scaling.
  •  Manufacturing Platform: In this model, production assets remain owned by independent suppliers, but a
  centralized platform provides the coordination layer that governs how those assets are utilized. The platform
  aggregates demand from multiple buyers and allocates work across a distributed supplier network, resulting
  in high coordination intensity driven by standardized execution protocols and real-time monitoring. Supplier
  onboarding, quality systems, procurement, and logistics are managed through shared digital infrastructure
  that provides system-wide visibility and control. The capital architecture remains asset-light at the platform
  level, with growth achieved by aggregating and optimizing existing supplier capacity rather than through
  direct ownership of manufacturing assets. Rather than functioning as a passive marketplace, the platform
  operates as an orchestration layer for distributed manufacturing, synchronizing capacity, financing, and
  delivery across a multi-tier network.
  We are a technology-led, asset-light manufacturing platform driving production of industrial goods, both in India and
  globally. We aggregate manufacturing capacity across a network of third-party suppliers and our own facilities into a
  “universal factory," unified through Zetwerk OS, our proprietary technology backbone. Our customers range across
  sizes, from innovative startups to mature companies, and across industries, including utilities, renewables, consumer
  electronics, artificial intelligence infrastructure, aerospace, space & defence, oil & gas, and industrial automation.
  Accordingly, there are no listed companies in India or globally which operate in a similar business model as ours.

Our Journey: The Evolution of Our Manufacturing Platform
Our Company was incorporated in December 2017. Our Promoters, Amrit Pratik Acharya and Srinath Ramakkrushnan, bring
experience in manufacturing sourcing from their previous professional roles at ITC.
In our early stages, we operated as a pure manufacturing marketplace, with demand sourced entirely from India and supplied
exclusively through Indian suppliers. Our initial focus was on aggregating suppliers for capital goods products that were
typically project‑based and non‑recurring in nature. For example, customers setting up greenfield manufacturing facilities
would engage us to source machinery or machine components. Such orders were generally one‑time, with limited follow‑on
requirements other than spare parts.
Over time, we intentionally diversified the platform across multiple growth vectors, including:
•  expanding beyond India to serve international customers;
•  supplying recurring, opex‑linked components in addition to project‑based capex orders;
•  adding design‑led capabilities by offering original design manufacturing (“ODM”) solutions for select customers and
  components;
•  expanding our supplier base internationally and also to include a broader range of manufacturing processes capable
  of serving multiple industries; and
•  selectively establishing our own manufacturing facilities.
As a result, we have evolved from an India‑only marketplace catering to limited industries into a diversified and resilient
manufacturing platform with multiple demand engines, a multi-geography supplier base and an expanded capability for
customers in India and internationally.

Overview
We are a technology-led, asset-light manufacturing platform for industrial and consumer goods in India and globally. We
aggregate manufacturing capacity across a network of third-party suppliers and our own facilities into a “universal factory,"
unified through Zetwerk OS, our proprietary technology backbone. Our customers range across sizes, from innovative startups
to mature companies, and across industries, including utilities, renewables, consumer electronics, artificial intelligence
infrastructure, aerospace, space & defence, oil & gas, and industrial automation. Some of our customers include Siemens
Gamesa, Acer India Private Limited, boAt, NextPower (formerly Nextracker), CG Power and Industrial Solutions Limited,
NTPC Renewable Energy Limited, L&T MHI Power Boiler Pvt Ltd, DRDO (Electronics & Radar Development Establishment
(LRDE)), National Aluminium Company Limited, Indian Oil Corporation Limited, Mortenson, Nordex Acciona, Indian Air
Force, and Numaligarh Refinery Limited. Third-party suppliers with whom we work have capabilities in manufacturing
processes, including sheet metal stamping, metal fabrication, injection moulding, extrusions, assembly, die casting, forging,
grinding and machining, and span several countries, including industrial geographies such as India, Vietnam, Thailand, Taiwan,
Germany, Spain, Eastern Europe, Mexico and the USA. Since our incorporation, we have cumulatively delivered products and
raw materials worth ₹743,742.10 million in terms of GMV to our customers.
Our Business Model
Our business model is centred around our technology‑led, asset‑light manufacturing platform, through which we enable our
customers to source diversified industrial products. We operate primarily as a principal, contracting directly with customers for
the supply of manufactured goods and related services, while procuring such goods and services from third‑party suppliers and,
in certain cases, executing specific activities through our own facilities. We have two operating segments:
•  Manufacturing Business segment: comprises design, manufacturing, supply and/or installation of custom-made goods
  focused on sectors of energy, precision components, capital goods and other ancillaries. This would include products
  sourced/ manufactured at third-party facilities and accounted in the books as procurement and sale of products.
  Additionally, this would also include projects with scope of design/ engineering and construction/ installation.

Manufacturing Business
Customer orders
We generate revenue in our Manufacturing Business through customer orders for manufactured products and related services.
Customers place one-time, or multi-year orders with us for sourcing of products, in the form of purchase orders, master supply
agreements or long‑term arrangements, including multi‑year take‑or‑pay contracts in certain cases.
Upon receipt and acceptance, customer orders are logged into our order book, which represents the aggregate value of confirmed
but unexecuted orders as of a given date. Orders in the order book are executed over time, depending on product specifications,
production schedules, logistics timelines and contractual milestones. Revenue is recognised as and when performance
obligations under the relevant contracts are satisfied.
In the case of take‑or‑pay arrangements, customers commit to procuring a minimum value or volume of goods over a specified
period. If the customer does not place orders up to the committed threshold within the stipulated time, they are contractually
obligated to pay an agreed margin or shortfall amount. Such arrangements provide greater revenue visibility and reduce demand
volatility, although actual revenue recognised remains linked to execution of goods and services or enforceable contractual
payments.
Execution of orders from suppliers
Once an order is confirmed, we deploy our proprietary technology platform to match the order with suitable manufacturers
from our panel of third-party suppliers based on factors such as capability, capacity, location, quality track record, and delivery
timelines. Under this model:
•  We procure manufactured goods from third-party suppliers on a principal-to-principal basis;
•  We may split a single customer order across multiple suppliers where appropriate to optimise timelines or capacity
  utilisation;
  564
•  In certain cases, we also utilise our own facilities for assembly, finishing or specific manufacturing processes.
We take title to the goods procured from suppliers before supplying them to customers, and bear responsibility for quality
assurance, delivery and contractual performance.
Ecosystem Business
Under our Ecosystem Business segment, which we operate under the brand Terra91, we facilitate the sourcing and supply of
industrial raw materials and commodities to customers.
Under Terra91, our customers place purchase orders with us for sourcing of raw materials such as ferrous and non-ferrous
metals and other industrial commodities used in manufacturing processes. We aggregate these purchase orders and buy the
materials in bulk from our panel of suppliers and manufacturers, leveraging aggregated demand to optimise pricing, availability,
and delivery timelines.
We act as a principal to customers, purchasing raw materials from suppliers and supplying them to customers. Revenue is
recognised upon transfer of control of the materials to the customer and margins are derived from the spread between
procurement costs and customer pricing, taking into account logistics, handling and related services.
While Terra91 originated as a sourcing solution to support suppliers in our Manufacturing Business, it has evolved into a
standalone revenue segment serving third-party customers across multiple industries.
