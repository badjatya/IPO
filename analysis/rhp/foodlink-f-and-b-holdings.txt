a result of inflation, seasonality, weather, supply and demand in local and international markets, economic conditions and other
factors which are beyond our control. Majority of the raw materials used by us are perishable in nature. Increase in the cost of
important raw materials could significantly increase our expenses.

We have historically been able to pass on a significant portion of increased raw material and operating costs to our customers.
Our scale of operations enables us to negotiate discounts and other favourable terms on contracts with our suppliers. Such

maintain a number of suppliers for each of our key ingredients, which we believe can help mitigate price volatility. Given the
increase in the number of events catered to and our restaurants and scale of operations, we have increased our efforts to centralise
supply sources and consequently, enhance our bargaining power with suppliers.
We have no control over fluctuations in the price and availability of ingredients or variations in products caused by external
factors. If we are not able to obtain requisite quantities of quality ingredients at commercially reasonable prices, our ability to
provide the menu items that are central to our business, or as required by our customers whose events we cater to, would be
adversely affected. In addition, any shortage of raw materials in the market generally could impact the prices charged by our
suppliers, making the cost of raw materials more expensive for us. This in turn could result in an increase in prices to our
customers which may reduce demand and therefore adversely affect our business, financial condition, results of operations and
prospects. In addition, we purchase electricity, oil, natural gas and other related supplies needed to operate our Casual Dining
Restaurants, Cloud Kitchens and Centralized Kitchens. Any significant increase in energy costs could adversely affect us
through higher rates or imposition of fuel surcharges by our suppliers.
We cannot predict whether we will be able to anticipate and react favourably to changing costs by adjusting our purchasing
practices and pricing, and a failure to do so could adversely affect our business, financial condition, results of operations and
prospects. In addition, we may be unable to fully pass on price increases to our customers. In addition, if we are unable to adjust
our significant fixed costs including lease costs and staff costs, in response to a reduction in revenues in the future, our business,
financial condition, results of operations and prospects could be adversely affected.
Consumer spending and general economic and market conditions
Our success depends to a significant extent on consumer spending patterns, which is influenced by general economic conditions
and discretionary income levels. Many factors affect the level of consumer confidence and spending, including recession,
inflation, deflation, political uncertainty, availability of consumer credit, taxation, stock market performance and
unemployment. Our performance may decline during recessionary periods or in other periods where one or more
macroeconomic factors, or potential macroeconomic factors, negatively affect the level of consumer confidence and spending,
including the amount that consumers spend on luxury events and restaurant dining.
As per the F&S Report, India’s wedding market stands as one of the most robust sectors globally, deeply intertwined with the
nation’s cultural ethos and economic dynamics. With 4.86-6.62 million weddings annually and a wedding spend of ₹8,000–
16,000 billion, the Indian wedding market is not just a cultural phenomenon but a significant driver of discretionary spending.
A significant portion of spending in luxury weddings goes toward catering and hospitality. Catering accounts for 18–20% of
the overall budget, often involves gourmet regional cuisines, fusion dishes, and live food counters that add an interactive
element to the dining experience. India’s Casual Dining Restaurant (CDR) and Fine Dining Restaurant (FDR) segments have
emerged as pivotal players in the evolving foodservice market, driven by rising disposable incomes, aspirational consumption,
and an increasing preference for experiential dining.
Unfavourable changes in the above factors or in other business and economic conditions affecting our customers could increase
our costs, impose practical limits on the pricing of our products, any of which could lower our profit margins and have an
adverse effect on our business, results of operations and financial condition.
