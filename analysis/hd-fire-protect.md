## What the company does
Mumbai-based maker of fire-protection equipment and systems: sprinklers & alarm valves (34% of FY26 revenue),
deluge valves/skids & pre-action systems (22%), foam suppression (19%), water-spray nozzles, monitors, system valves,
gas suppression. Per CRISIL (paid for by the company) it is India's **#2 fire-protection equipment maker and the largest
exporter**; holds 21 UL and 87 FM certifications, the most among Indian peers, which is a real entry barrier (certification
takes 6–24 months, vendor empanelment 3–12 months). Two plants in Maharashtra (Jalgaon, Thane). ~65% domestic / ~35% export
(UAE, Saudi, Brazil, Turkey, Malaysia, Indonesia). Marquee sites: Sriharikota launch pad, Jamnagar refinery, new Parliament,
Saudi Aramco Ras Tanura. Order book ₹158.6 Cr (Jun-26) vs ₹70.3 Cr (Mar-25).

## Promoters & ownership

generation Miheer Ghotikar, Parika Ghotikar, Anik Dharamshi (each ~16 yrs in the business, whole-time directors).
- Promoter holding: **95.3% pre-issue → ~80.3% post-issue**
- Sellers: Kusum Dharamshi 1.73 Cr shares (~₹469 Cr), Harish Dharamshi 0.90 Cr shares (~₹244 Cr)
- Most promoter shares came from bonus issues (2023, 2024, 2025 10:1) → near-zero acquisition cost
- No PE/VC investors; no change in control in 5 years

## Red flags
- 100% OFS + ₹196 Cr of dividends in the two years before the IPO → promoters monetising, no growth capital raised
- Earnings growth decelerating (FY26 PAT +6%), Q1 FY27 margins lower, inventory build-up
- Customer count falling (2,179 → 2,066) even as revenue grows
- ~35% export revenue with Middle East concentration; product-liability risk if equipment fails
- Industry data from a company-paid CRISIL report; key-person dependence on a 72-year-old founder

## Verdict
**Apply — for listing gains; long-term hold is optional.**
1. Quality niche franchise: certification moat, debt-free, ~30% RoE, export leadership.
2. Priced below the peer average P/E despite better returns; GMP ₹83 (~31%) shows strong grey-market demand.
3. Large 35% retail quota improves retail allotment odds.
Long-term: good business at a full price; growth slowdown and pure-OFS structure argue for booking partial gains on listing.
